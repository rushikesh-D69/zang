# Zang v0.0.1 schematic review (firmware view)

Source: `Zang v0.0.1 Schmeatics.pdf` (3 sheets: power/camera/audio/servo, ESP32-P4, display/mic/ESP8684). Every GPIO
assignment below was extracted from the PDF's text coordinates: each net label sits on its pin row at offset 0.0, and
the pin number sits 2.2 units above. It was then checked against ESP-IDF v5.5.6 SoC headers (`soc/esp32p4/include/soc/*`)
and the ESP32-P4 datasheet v0.7 §3. Items marked **VERIFY** could not be settled from the PDF text alone. Check them in
the EDA project.

## Findings

1. **BLOCKER: the servo bus is not connected to the P4.** Nets `SS_P` and `SS_N` appear once each, only inside the
   "SIGNAL CIRCUIT" (100 Ω each to `SIGNAL`, 1 kΩ pull-ups to 3V3). No P4 pin carries them. Without them the robot cannot
   move.
   *v0.0.1 rework:* wire `SS_P` → **GPIO47** (UART TX, firmware sets it open-drain) and `SS_N` → **GPIO48** (UART RX).
   Both are free, not straps, and in the 3.3 V domain. *v0.0.2:* draw those connections, or use a real half-duplex
   buffer (74LVC1G125 + direction pin, as Dynamixel/Feetech boards do) if the bus has to run at 1 Mbaud over 15 servos.
2. **SD card is wired off the slot-0 IOMUX pins.** IDF `sdmmc_pins.h` gives slot 0 as CLK 43, CMD 44, D0 39, D1 40, D2 41,
   D3 42. The board has CLK 42, CMD 41, D0 43, D1 44, D2 39, D3 40. *Works:* the P4 can route **slot 1 through the GPIO
   matrix** (`SOC_SDMMC_USE_GPIO_MATRIX`), and firmware does that, at up to high-speed 40 MHz, which is plenty for
   actions and MP3s. *v0.0.2:* re-order to the IOMUX pins (needed only for UHS-I/SDR50+).
3. **C2 UART is "straight", not crossed, by name.** Net `TX` joins P4 GPIO26 to C2 `U0TXD`, and net `RX` joins P4 GPIO27
   to C2 `U0RXD`. Firmware therefore uses **P4 RX = GPIO26, P4 TX = GPIO27** (GPIO matrix). This is not a fault, but rename
   the nets `C2_TX`/`C2_RX` so nobody "fixes" it the wrong way.
   Consequence: ESP-Hosted runs over **UART** (the C2 has no SDIO, and SPI isn't wired). Plan on about 3 Mbaud, which
   is roughly 250–300 kB/s of real Wi-Fi throughput. That is ample for ZLink (STATE 50 Hz × 98 B ≈ 5 kB/s) and for
   16 kHz PCM audio (32 kB/s), but tight for camera video (QVGA JPEG ≈ 8–12 kB/frame → ~10–15 fps at best).
   *v0.0.2 if video streaming matters:* wire SPI (4 signals + handshake + data-ready) between P4 and C2.
4. **Camera PWDN (connector pin 17) has no net: it floats.** On OV2640 PWDN = 1 means power-down, and a floating pin can
   randomly blank the sensor. *Rework:* tie PWDN to GND (or to a free GPIO, e.g. GPIO49, with a pull-down).
   Camera RESET is only pulled up (R18 10 kΩ to 2.8 V). That's acceptable: firmware resets the sensor over SCCB
   (COM7 = 0x80).
5. **No IMU on this board** (Zing had a QMI8658). Firmware keeps the `imu` component optional (off). Consequences: no fall
   detection, and the robot can't sense when it is lifted or pushed. Any learned balance policy also loses its most
   important input (see `ai_stack/rl/README.md`). *v0.0.2:* add an IMU on the existing I2C bus (GPIO17/18): QMI8658C or
   ICM-42688-P, plus its INT pin to a free GPIO (e.g. GPIO50), mounted near the pelvis/torso centre.
6. **VERIFY TFT RESET (FPC pin 11):** no net label on its row. If it floats, the panel can reset at random. Tie it to
   3V3 through 10 kΩ with 1 µF to GND (power-on reset), or better to a free GPIO (e.g. GPIO51). The firmware sends a
   software reset (SWRESET) in every case.
   **VERIFY LCD_CS:** the `LCD_CS` label sits on the pin-6 (GND) row of the FPC, not the CS (pin 8) row. Check that CS
   really lands on pin 8.
7. **VERIFY PDM mic L/R straps:** both mics share CLK/DATA, so one must have L/R = GND and the other L/R = VDD. If both
   are strapped the same, they drive the data line at the same time.
8. **VERIFY NS4168 CTRL (pin 1):** it should be tied high (≥1.5 V, left channel, amp always on). If it floats, the amp
   may stay in shutdown. Optional for v0.0.2: put CTRL on a GPIO for a software mute (stops idle hiss and pops).
9. No TE (tearing-effect) pin on the display FPC. Firmware can't sync to panel refresh, so it uses partial
   (dirty-region) updates and keeps animation ≤ panel refresh. Brief tearing during full-screen motion is possible.
10. Servo power: four connectors on the raw 2S pack (8.4 V) through 7 A PTCs. That matches the DS-R003 family (6–8.4 V).
    A DS-R003 stalls at up to 5 A, so 3–4 servos per chain can trip a 7 A PTC under load (e.g. a squat). Size the
    battery and PTCs for the realistic peak, not the rated current.

## Strapping pins (ESP32-P4 datasheet v0.7 §3): correct

| Strap | Board | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| GPIO35 (boot) | 10 kΩ up + BOOT button | SPI boot; button → download |
| GPIO36 | 10 kΩ up (R24) | needed = 1 for joint download mode; also enables ROM log on UART0 |
| GPIO34 (JTAG source) | floating | default (USB-JTAG) |
| GPIO37 / GPIO38 | floating | allowed ("any value") |
| CHIP_PU (EN_P4) | 10 kΩ + 1 µF, button | RC ≈ 10 ms ≥ hold time t_H = 3 ms ✓ |

No camera, LCD or audio pin is on a strap, so their peripherals cannot change the boot mode.
C2 straps: GPIO9 = BOOT_C2 (10 kΩ up, driven by P4 GPIO28) and GPIO8 has 10 kΩ up (required for download). CHIP_EN has
10 kΩ + 1 µF and is driven by P4 GPIO29. ✓

## Camera timing (DVP)

The sensor is assumed to be an **OV2640** (24-pin DVP, DVDD 1.2 V via ME6211A12, AVDD 2.8 V via ME6206A28, DOVDD 3.3 V).
**VERIFY** the module part number; GC2145 and OV3660 need different DVDD rails.
- XCLK 20 MHz from the P4 DVP controller on GPIO13 (OV2640 accepts 6–27 MHz; 20 MHz is the esp32-camera default and
  divides cleanly from the 160 MHz and 40 MHz sources).
- 8-bit data: sensor Y2..Y9 → CAM_D0..D7. VSYNC active-high, HREF = DE active-high, data sampled on PCLK rising edge.
  These match the OV2640 defaults (COM10 = 0x00).
- Modes: QVGA/VGA RGB565 or YUV422 for on-board vision (15–30 fps); JPEG for sending over Wi-Fi (limited by finding 3).
- SCCB on I2C GPIO17/18 at 100 kHz (OV2640 SCCB max 400 kHz).

## Display frame rate (SPI TFT)

The SPI clock comes from SPLL 480 MHz through integer dividers, so the usable LCD clocks are 40, 48, 60 and 80 MHz. The
LCD is write-only, so the GPIO-matrix input-delay limit does not apply. The ceiling is the panel: ST7789 needs a write
cycle of ≥ 16 ns (62.5 MHz), so 60 MHz is the highest clock in spec. GC9A01 and ILI9341 are usually fine at 40–60 MHz
over a short FPC.

| Panel | Bits / full frame (RGB565) | 40 MHz | 60 MHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| 240×240 (ST7789 1.3–1.54″ / GC9A01 round) | 921 600 | ≈ 41 fps | ≈ 62 fps |
| 240×280 | 1 075 200 | ≈ 35 fps | ≈ 53 fps |
| 240×320 (ST7789 2″ / ILI9341) | 1 228 800 | ≈ 31 fps | ≈ 46 fps |

These are about 95 % of the theoretical rate (DMA plus window-command overhead). Firmware defaults: 60 MHz for ST7789,
40 MHz otherwise (Kconfig), two DMA line buffers in internal RAM, full-frame buffer in PSRAM, partial updates for the
eyes/face (only the changed rectangles are sent, so 60 fps is easy), and a target of 30 fps for full-screen animation.
The backlight on GPIO23 drives the AO3401A P-MOSFET gate, so it is **active-low**: LEDC PWM inverted, at 20 kHz to avoid
audible whine.
